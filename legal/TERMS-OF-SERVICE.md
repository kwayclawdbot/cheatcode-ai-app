## What I most want a lawyer to read

1. **Section 3 and the box inside it — "private, members-only" versus the open
   sign-up screen that actually ships.** This is the single most likely thing in the
   whole submission to be contradicted by a reviewer within two minutes of opening
   the app. It is a factual problem before it is a legal one, but it lands in the
   terms.
2. **Section 4 against Apple Guideline 3.1.3(b).** The claim "all subscriptions are
   purchased on our website, not in the app" is only true if the app's Upgrade button
   and its web-checkout call are removed from the submitted build. They exist in the
   code today. A lawyer should also confirm the app qualifies for the multiplatform
   exception at all, rather than needing In-App Purchase.
3. **Section 9, the liability cap, in a trading context.** A user who loses money
   after reading a graded setup in this app is the scenario the cap exists for. It
   deserves more than a template sentence.
4. **Section 2 and the boundary between "education" and "investment advice".** The
   app publishes graded setups with entries, stops, targets and position sizes. In
   several jurisdictions that is close to, or over, the line into regulated advice
   or a regulated publication, regardless of what the disclaimer says. **This is the
   question I would spend the money on.**
5. **Section 5, and whether the platform-liability protections the app relies on for
   user content actually apply** to a service that also generates content of its own.
6. **Section 12, arbitration and venue**, and whether the terms are enforceable in
   every territory the app is listed in. Restricting territories may be cheaper than
   complying everywhere.
